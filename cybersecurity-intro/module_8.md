### information

- digital information and data assests covers everything that can be stored, processed, or transmitted through digital systems.

*risk management*

- asessesing the value of information assets belonging to an individual or organization and protects them on an ongoing basis.

*imperatives & incentives*

- imperatives are pressure that force you to act.
- incentives are rewards & opportunities that arise from acting.

- The pressures for information security stems from legislation & regulation. 
  - e.g. The Computer Misuse Act & the Data Protection Act 2018. (UK's implementation of General Data Protection Regulation(GDPR))

- The most important incentive is *trust*
- establishing this trust would secure the examination of each other's information security practices that ensure there's safeguards to protect the information.
  - e.g. standards for designing and implementing information security management system. (*PCI-DSS or ISO27000*)

### Checklist for staying safe online 

- out of date software requires an update because of the increased likelihood of vulnerability. 
- newer OS will have fixes that operate/contain bugs. 
- Applications (i.e. microsoft office, java programming, and web browsers) require regular updating to fix security problems. 
- installing a free linux OS on older equipment will provide a update in software 
- basic check list:
  1. set up personal firewall
  2. installing antivirus program (unless you have a Mac, which has certain security features like XProtect & MRT/XProtect Remediator, which indicates if the individual has malicious code.)
  3. get used to making backups.
  4. requiring passwords to login
  5. set up two-factor authentication for social media & financial sites
  6. unique strong password for each website
  7. using a password manager or encrypting password folder
  8. using a hard disk encryption (especially on laptops.)

### Web browser security

- using certain alternatives to create a better environment in terms of security:
  - using a secure VPN or TOR browsers when using public Wi-Fi.
  - using a search engine such as duckduckgo (doesn't track you)
  - using a browser such as Brave, designed to put your security first.
- "turning off" third party cookies, which helps in understanding that it's used by websites that host cookies belonging to organizations that to track your use of the web and websites.

*Countermeasures for risk management*

- avoiding the risk - avoidance means stopping the activity that's causing the risk.
- modifying the risk - choosing and implementing a mechanism that reduces the likelihood of an successful attack.
- transferring the risk to others - taking out insurances to cover the losses in the event threat materalises.
- accepting the risk - chosing to *NOT* implement any of the countermeasures.

### New Developments in CyberSecurity

- recent cybersecurity attacks that required more modern use of technology solutions:

*SamSam*

- Atlanta, Georgia faced a cyber attack which made parts of the governments inoperable. 
- It was ransomware, which  was in the amount of $51,0000 payable in 7 days  in the form of cryptocurrency.
- This prevented citizens of Atlanta not having the ability to pay basics services, not accepting employment options, business licenses weren't issued, cour warrants couldn't be validated, and the city's police department, which prevented officers to access thier computer systems. The police department had to handwrite police reports. And the wireless network of the international airport was rendered useless and had been shut down.
- the infected computer ran on Microsft's remote desktop protocol, allowing users to connect to other computers over a network.
- the recovery plan costed the city of atlanta over $2.6 million in extra staffing, additional computer infrastructure from Microsoft, consulting fees, and emergency communications.
